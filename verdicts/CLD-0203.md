# CLD-0203: First-time sign-in properties: ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 1 · 2026-08-25
Verdict: Insufficient data · Severity (my rating): Medium (reported Low)
QA review: Satisfactory

## Alert
Rule “First-time sign-in properties (guest account)” fired from Identity Monitoring for ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io (Eleanor Walsh, Bexley Payroll Integrations).

The triggering event was a successful sign-in at 21:47 UTC from 198.51.100.77 using macOS/Safari, with no MFA challenge due to the Bexley guest-access policy.

During review of the account's sign-in activity, a separate unusual login was also identified from 192.0.2.146, using Windows/Chrome and associated with unknown/hosting infrastructure, which differs from Eleanor's documented 198.51.100.77 and macOS/Safari activity.

## Hypothesis
Malicious: Eleanor Walsh's guest credentials may have been compromised, allowing an unauthorized person to access Cloudora through an account that does not require MFA. The unusual 21:47 sign-in could represent unauthorized access to the Bexley integration environment.

Benign: Eleanor is a legitimate Bexley consultant working during Bexley go-live week and may simply be working outside normal business hours. The sign-in could be legitimate activity from her normal device and network, with no MFA challenge because the existing Bexley guest policy does not require MFA.

## Evidence checked
Sign-in Logs: Reviewed Eleanor Walsh’s (ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io) sign-in history. Her established activity used 198.51.100.77, London, macOS/Safari. The triggering 21:47 UTC event matched these previously observed properties.

Additional Anomalous Sign-in: During review, identified a separate sign-in from 192.0.2.146 using Windows/Chrome and associated with unknown/hosting infrastructure. These properties differ from Eleanor’s established sign-in pattern.

IP Correlation: Searched 192.0.2.146 across the available sign-in logs. This appeared to be the only sign-in associated with that IP, providing no previous history establishing it as a known or trusted source.

Morning Handover: Confirmed Eleanor is an authorized consultant for Bexley Payroll Integrations and that Bexley go-live work is occurring this week. The handover also confirms that Eleanor’s guest account does not require MFA because the Bexley guest-access policy predates the current MFA baseline.

Help Desk Logs: Reviewed for additional context involving Eleanor/Bexley. No matching Help Desk record was identified.

Proxy/Egress Logs: Additional review identified later network activity associated with ewalsh.ext. On Oct. 7 at 11:02 UTC, an Eleanor session accessed 34 documents in the Payroll-Mapping SharePoint library over approximately 25 minutes. On Oct. 8 at 23:52 UTC, another ewalsh.ext session performed OAuth consent for “Bexley Sync Utility.” The proxy log does not identify the originating source IP for either session, so these events cannot currently be correlated directly to the anomalous 192.0.2.146 sign-in.

Evidence Gap: Additional session-level correlation is required to determine whether 192.0.2.146 was responsible for the SharePoint or OAuth activity and whether Eleanor authorized the session.

## Action
Request confirmation from Eleanor Walsh or the Bexley engagement owner on whether the 192.0.2.146 Windows/Chrome sign-in was legitimate.

Request/review the activity associated with that session, especially any access to the Bexley-Integration SharePoint site, to determine whether client data was accessed.

No containment performed yet because malicious activity has not been confirmed.

Escalation trigger: Escalate immediately to the vCISO if Eleanor denies the sign-in, evidence confirms unauthorized access to client/payroll data, or the activity cannot be bounded with the available data.

## Justification
Original Alert: The triggering 21:47 UTC sign-in from 198.51.100.77 matches Eleanor’s previously observed IP, London location, macOS, and Safari activity.

Shift Context: Eleanor is an authorized Bexley consultant, and Bexley go-live is occurring this week, providing a legitimate explanation for after-hours activity.

MFA: No MFA challenge occurred, but the Morning Handover confirms this is a known limitation of the existing Bexley guest-access policy and does not by itself prove MFA bypass or account compromise.

Anomalous Activity: Investigation identified a separate sign-in from 192.0.2.146 using Windows/Chrome and hosting infrastructure, which differs from Eleanor’s established sign-in pattern.

IP History: 192.0.2.146 appeared only once in the available sign-in logs, so there is no previous activity establishing this source as legitimate.

Additional Correlation: Proxy/egress evidence identified later ewalsh.ext activity involving 34 documents in the Payroll-Mapping SharePoint library and an OAuth consent for “Bexley Sync Utility.” However, the available proxy evidence does not identify the source IP of those sessions, so they cannot currently be attributed to 192.0.2.146.

Verdict: Insufficient Data. The original alert has several legitimate indicators, but the anomalous 192.0.2.146 session and later account activity cannot currently be confirmed as authorized or malicious.

Severity: Medium due to the possible impact to a guest account with access to Bexley integration/client data, while compromise remains unconfirmed.

Next Step: Obtain confirmation from Eleanor/Bexley and correlate the anomalous authentication session with SharePoint/OAuth activity. Escalate to the vCISO if Eleanor denies the session, unauthorized client/payroll data access is confirmed, or the activity cannot be bounded with the available evidence.

## Tuning note
Recommend reviewing the “First-time sign-in properties” rule to baseline guest accounts against their established IP, device, browser, and sign-in history rather than relying heavily on unusual login time alone.

The rule should continue alerting on new IPs, devices, browsers, or hosting-provider sources, especially for guest accounts without MFA.

Risk: Over-tuning after-hours activity could hide a compromised guest account being used outside normal business hours.
